# Prompts You Can Copy and Paste

Each prompt is written for a small, boring task. Replace the parts in
[square brackets] with your own details, and keep the rule at the end:
ask before sending, spending, deleting, or changing anything outside this chat.

## 1. Morning inbox triage

> Go through my inbox from the last 24 hours. Pull out only security alerts,
> bills, support replies, and things that expire soon. Skip marketing mail.
> Give me a short list with one line per item and a link or source for each.
> If nothing needs my attention, just say so. Do not send, archive, or delete
> anything without asking me first.

## 2. Research brief

> Compare [option A] and [option B] for [purpose]. Check current prices and
> key specs from the sources themselves, and tell me which source each fact
> came from. If you cannot verify something, say so plainly instead of
> guessing. End with a one-paragraph recommendation and one reason it could
> be wrong.

## 3. Read the long document

> Read [document or link] and give me only the parts that affect [decision].
> Keep it under 300 words, quote the exact numbers and dates, and link to
> the sections they came from. Flag anything that looks outdated or
> contradictory.

## 4. Messy notes into a document

> Turn these notes into a clean [summary / plan / comparison table]:
>
> [paste your notes here]
>
> Keep the facts as written. Do not invent dates, names, or numbers. Mark
> anything unclear with [needs checking] instead of filling it in. I will
> edit before I send it.

## 5. Meeting notes into action items

> Turn these meeting notes into a list of decisions and action items. For
> each action item, include who owns it, what exactly needs doing, and the
> deadline if one was stated. If no owner or deadline was stated, leave it
> blank and flag it. Do not notify anyone about these items.

## 6. Watchdog check

> Check [price / page / inbox thread] and tell me only if it has changed
> since your last check. If it has changed, show me the old value, the new
> value, and the source. If nothing changed, just say "no change" and stop.

## 7. Weekly review

> Review what I handed you this week and list: (1) tasks that saved me real
> time, (2) tasks where your first draft needed a correction, and (3) one
> task I should stop handing you or hand you differently. Be specific and
> use examples from this week.

## 8. Draft a reply I will check first

> Draft a reply to [person / thread] about [topic]. Keep it short, polite,
> and factual. Do not send it. I will read it, edit it, and send it myself.
> If any fact in the draft is uncertain, mark it clearly.

## How to use these well

- Start with one prompt, not all eight. The morning inbox prompt alone is a
  good first week.
- If a result looks too confident, ask: "Which source did that fact come
  from, and what could be wrong with it?"
- If a prompt keeps producing the same mistake, change the prompt, not just
  the answer. Add the exact check you want, for example: "Quote the date
  from the source, do not rephrase it."
